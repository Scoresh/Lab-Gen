Enzymes have four factors that affect rate of reaction. Each specific enzyme works best at specific temperatures and pH levels. Generally, rising temperature speeds up a reaction, but extreme heat may cause an enzyme to denature. Additionally, each enzyme has an ideal pH range. Extreme pH values may cause enzymes to denature. Denaturing occurs when the enzyme loses its shape due to these stressing factors. This lab is explores denaturing of a specific enzyme, amylase, via acidity.

\section{Question / Problem}\label{question-problem}

How does \textbf{\emph{amylase}}, an enzyme found in saliva, break polysaccharides into simpler carbohydrates in different PH levels?

\section{Elements \& Hypothesis}\label{elements-hypothesis}

\subsection{Hypothesis}\label{hypothesis}

If the pH levels of three solutions of pH \{4, 5.5, 7\}, are exposed to the enzyme \emph{amylase} found in saliva and mixed with a polysaccharide containing cracker, then there should be a trend whereas the pH 7 solution will have the highest density of glucose, the pH 5.5 solution should have the medium density of glucose, and the pH 4 solution should have the lowest density of glucose.

\section{Methods / Procedure}\label{methods-procedure}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  Saliva was collected by each individual labmate into their respective plastic test tube. This was to minimize the safety impact of having different contaminants on each test tube.
\item
  Four crackers (approximately 1 gram each) were crushed by a mortar and pestile into a fine powder.
\item
  The four crackers were distributed evenly across the \textbf{second} set of test tubes. (So now, the second set only has cracker powder in it.)
\item
  The saliva was mixed into a fourth plastic test tube and vortexed via the Genie 2 Vortex Mixer
\item
  The labmates were careful here; the saliva was then distributed evenly into the \textbf{first} set of test tubes. (So now, the first set only has solution + saliva. Only \(\frac{3}{4}\) contained saliva because of the negative control.)
\item
  Carefully, the first set of test tubes was added onto each respective test tube of the second set and began a stopwatch.
\item
  This is where the rush began. During the 30 seconds of curing (determined via the stopwatch), each test tube was vortexed to ensure that each solution was at least somewhat homogenous. This was a step where caution was taken to ensure that mixture escaped the test tube.
\item
  After the 30 seconds of curing (determined via the stopwatch) was over, one urineanalysis strip was placed into each test tube to allow for glucose measurement.
\item
  The stopwatch was reset and began counting to 30 seconds again. After the 30 seconds were over, each urineanalysis strip was carefully removed from each test tube. Then, the urineanalysis strip was compared to the package, and the glucose levels were measured and recorded.
\end{enumerate}

The lab results support the hypothesis. Both control groups (Positive, Negative) behaved as expected. Since the negative control does not contain saliva, it shouldn\textquotesingle t have amylase. This removed sources of possible error from faulty urinanalysis strips. Additionally, the positive control group behaved as expected, resulting in the highest concentration of glucose. Since the optimal pH level for amylase is 7.0 \textbf{INSERT CITATION HERE} , it is within reason to correlate the two. The two experimental groups behaved as expected. The acidic pH 4.0 solution had similar results to the negative control because at a pH of 4.0, amylase denatures \textbf{INSERT CITATION HERE}. The less acidic pH 5.5 solution also behaved as expected, yielding in a result greater than 0 but less than the positive control group. This, hence, means that the hypothesis can be accepted.

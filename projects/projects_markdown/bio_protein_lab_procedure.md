# Abstract
To show the denaturing of amylase through the change in pH levels and how the efficacy and efficiency of enzyme performance are scale-based.

# Background / Introduction

# Question / Problem

How does ***amylase***, an enzyme found in saliva, break polysaccharides into simpler carbohydrates in different PH levels? 

# Elements & Hypothesis

## Hypothesis
If the pH levels of three solutions: {4, 5.5, 7}, are exposed to the enzyme *amylase* found in saliva and mixed with a cracker, then there should be a trend whereas the pH 7 solution will have the highest density of glucose, the pH 5.5 solution should have the medium density of glucose, and the pH 4 solution should have the lowest density of glucose.

## Experimental Elements
- Independent Variable: PH Level of the solution
- Dependent Variable: Glucose concentration produced (in mmol/L)
- Experimental Groups:
    - Group 1: Saltine Cracker, Saliva, pH 4.0 solution
    - Group 2: Saltine Cracker, Saliva, pH 5.5 solution
- Positive Control Group:
    - Saltine Cracker, Saliva, optimal pH solution (pH 7.0)
- Negative Control Group:
    - Saltine Cracker, optimal pH solution (pH 7.0)
- Controls
    - Saliva to Solution ratio
    - Cracker to Solution ratio
    - Time allowed for enzymatic processes
    - Method of glucose measurement

# Materials / Set up
## Materials

The following materials were gathered:

1. 4 saltine cracker pieces (approximately 1 gram each)
2. Roughly 100 mL of Heinz all-natural vinegar 
3. pH test strips
4. Roughly 250 mL of distilled water
5. 8 15-25mL test tubes
6. Test tube rack
7. Life2O Pet Urinalysis Test Strips: Dog and Cat Urine Test Strips
8. Genie Vortex-Genie 2 Vortex Mixer SI-0236
9. Fresh Human Saliva (Roughly 20 mL total)
10. Stopwatch
11. Plastic pipettes
12. 3 stirring rods
13. Plastic gloves
14. Tape
15. Black Marker
16. 250 mL beaker

## Set Up Procedure

1. All lab materials were collected and thoroughly inspected to ensure cleanliness.
2. Two sets of four test tubes were labeled with {"C","pH=7","pH=5.5","pH=4"} using tape and a marker.
3. The first set of four test tubes were filled with 10 mL of distilled water. 
4. Then, according to the test tube labels, the first set had 2 mL of vinegar added incrementally and checked via a pH strip.
5. When all four test tubes of the first set were at the pH level indicated by each respective label, the tubes were put away, in a row, back onto the test tube rack
